What storm damage actually looks like

A lot of storm damage is not visible from the ground, which is why a free roof-top inspection matters after any major weather. In West Michigan we most often find:

  • Wind damage: creased, lifted, or missing shingles, and exposed nail heads where the seal broke
  • Hail damage: bruised spots where the granules are knocked off, which shortens the roof's life even when it is not leaking yet
  • Ice damage: ice dams that back water up under the shingles at the eaves, rotting the decking and staining ceilings
  • Debris and tree damage: punctures and dented flashing, vents, and gutters

Insurance adjusters are trained to spot these. So are we. The difference is we are on your side of the ladder.

Storm & Insurance FAQ

The questions we hear after a storm

Does homeowners insurance cover roof storm damage?

Usually yes. Most homeowners policies cover sudden damage from wind, hail, and storms. What matters is that the damage is documented correctly and filed on time. We inspect for free and tell you honestly whether you have a claim worth filing before you ever call your insurer.

Will filing a claim raise my rates?

Storm and weather claims are usually treated differently than at-fault claims, and a single weather event often does not affect your rate the way people fear. We are not insurance agents, so we will always tell you to confirm with your provider, but we can walk you through what to expect before you decide.

What if my roof was already old?

An older roof can still have a valid storm claim if the storm caused new damage. The key is documenting the damage as storm-caused, not just wear. That is exactly what our roof-top inspection and photos are for.
